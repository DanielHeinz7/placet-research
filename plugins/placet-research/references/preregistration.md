# Hypotheses before data

Attached to elements 4 and 6 by the `is-quantitative` profile.

For confirmatory hypothesis testing, state the theory-derived hypotheses
(element 4) and the design with its analysis plan (element 6) before inspecting
results that could influence them. Exploratory findings can ground a research
model (Gregor & Klein 2014, §2.1). When hypotheses are generated after results
are known, identify that work as theory generation, not an a priori test
(Mertens & Recker 2020, HARKing discussion and accepted manuscript, Table 3).

Mertens & Recker's author-facing guidelines (2020, accepted manuscript,
Table 3) distinguish what researchers "Must not do", "Should do" and "Could
do". Researchers must not declare hypotheses as a priori if they were conceived
after data collection or analysis, and should distinguish a priori expectations
from post-hoc inferences. Declaring a paper theory-generating when hypothesizing
after results are known is a "Could do" option. Preregistering hypotheses is
also a "Could do" option, not a "Should do" recommendation; preregistering
protocols and instruments is listed under "Should do".

For this profile, record the hypotheses and the planned measures, sampling and
analysis choices before collecting new confirmatory data, or before analyzing
existing data for the planned test. Label any prior access to the data and
later deviations. This is the profile's documentation practice, not a claim
that the cited journals require this canvas workflow. Define constructs before
developing measures and plan their validation; preregistration does not by
itself establish measurement validity (MacKenzie et al. 2011, Figure 1).

`placet-research review` checks elements 4 and 6 separately under "Findings
before hypotheses". For each element, it reports when the first log entry on
element 7 has no earlier `DECISION` entry on that element. It blocks nothing.
The check compares log sequence numbers; it does not examine whether the
hypotheses or analysis plan are complete. If an early finding informed theory
generation, explain that history and distinguish it from a priori testing
(Gregor & Klein 2014, §2.1; Mertens & Recker 2020, HARKing discussion and
accepted manuscript, Table 3).

This is not preregistration with a registry or results-blind peer review. The
sealed log records entry order, and the authors can publish its anchor
(`placet-research review`, "Anchor"). It cannot establish when data were
collected, first accessed or analyzed outside the log. Acceptance before a
finding was logged therefore does not prove that the hypothesis preceded
knowledge of the results. Mertens & Recker (2020, accepted manuscript,
Table 2) separately propose community changes: enforcing preregistration of
hypotheses before data analysis and introducing results-blind journal review
before data collection. These proposals go beyond this check and differ from
the graded author-facing guidance in Table 3.
